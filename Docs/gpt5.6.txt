TRANCE — SOLO COMMERCIAL DEVELOPMENT ESTIMATE
Prepared: 10 August 2026

PURPOSE

Estimate the work for one full-time developer (40 hours/week) to turn the supplied
Godot prototype and design document into a commercial Windows game released on
itch.io. The estimate separates reusable/common production work from the cost of
simple and complex music-track levels and inventories the non-music art and audio
assets required.


EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Planning formula (before contingency):

  900–1,300 hours common work
  + 100–160 hours per simple song
  + 280–440 hours per complex song

Add 20% schedule contingency after selecting the song count and mix. This covers
normal solo-development uncertainty, iteration after playtests, integration bugs,
and modest rework; it does not cover a major redesign or delayed music rights.

The design says at least three songs—the first, last, and day-to-night transition—
receive complex treatment. Using exactly three complex songs gives:

  7 songs / about 1 hour:
    2,140–3,260 production hours before contingency
    2,568–3,912 hours including 20% contingency
    64–98 full-time weeks, approximately 15–23 working months

  14 songs / about 2 hours:
    2,840–4,380 production hours before contingency
    3,408–5,256 hours including 20% contingency
    85–131 full-time weeks, approximately 20–30 working months

  28 songs / about 4 hours:
    4,240–6,620 production hours before contingency
    5,088–7,944 hours including 20% contingency
    127–199 full-time weeks, approximately 29–46 working months

These are work-time conversions (52 working weeks = 12 months), not promises of
calendar ship dates. Vacations, illness, administration, contractor coordination,
and waiting for external assets extend calendar time. A practical first commercial
release is therefore the seven-song version: three showcase songs and four simple
songs. A sensible public target would be roughly 18–24 calendar months for a solo
developer, assuming the art style remains production-efficient and the music and
rights are ready on time.


SCOPE AND ASSUMPTIONS

Included:

* Windows PC build distributed through itch.io.
* Keyboard/mouse and common controller support.
* One connected 2D festival hub with entrance, hill/small lake, tents/desert,
  large lake/boat, stages, food area, and dressing/detail sufficient for release.
* Day, dusk, night, and dawn presentation states; gated conversations/areas/songs.
* Main cast, crowds, dialogue, tutorial, end sequence, credits, and basic narrative.
* No-fail rhythm play, timing feedback, score/grade, completion and retry flow.
* Auto-run, walking/looping, free-movement, freestyle, jumping and directional
  dash patterns described in the design.
* Automatic save after songs, settings, pause/main menus, remapping and basic
  accessibility options.
* Production tools/data formats so songs are authored as data rather than by
  repeatedly editing hard-coded scripts.
* Optimization, playtesting, bug fixing, packaging, itch.io page materials, and a
  small post-launch patch allowance.

Assumed constraints:

* The developer is already comfortable with Godot, GDScript, 2D content pipelines,
  and basic audio editing. Learning time would be additional.
* The developer also performs design, writing, implementation, level design,
  integration, production, and QA. Specialist-created art/audio time or cost is not
  estimated; the inventory below describes what must be made or commissioned.
* The final game stays close to the current pixel-art 2D approach and 640x360 base
  presentation. A high-frame-count hand-drawn style would substantially increase
  the asset budget and integration time.
* Music tracks and permission to use them are supplied. Each song can be delivered
  as a final master plus any loopable sections/stems needed for seamless transitions.
* English only. Localization infrastructure is included, but translating, editing,
  and testing other languages are not.
* No online services, multiplayer, console ports, achievements, workshop, accounts,
  or DRM. Signing and store requirements beyond itch.io are excluded.
* No voice acting and no bespoke musical SFX layered over rhythm levels, consistent
  with the design.
* A simple song uses the shared rhythm mechanics and reusable visual systems. A
  complex song has bespoke choreography, transitions, mechanics/set pieces, and a
  wordless story comparable in ambition to Chakra.
* Ratings/compliance, legal review, company setup, taxes, and marketing campaigns
  are outside development hours, except for preparation of basic itch.io materials.


WHAT THE CURRENT PROTOTYPE CONTRIBUTES

The inspected repository is a Godot 4.3 prototype with roughly 1,440 lines of
GDScript plus scenes and assets. It contains:

* character movement states for standing, walking, sprinting, jumping/falling, and
  rhythm-specific movement;
* a segmented audio playlist with automatic, looping, position-gated, and
  input-gated transitions;
* timestamped beat prompts, hit windows, directional prompts, hit counting, and a
  basic final score;
* synced drummer/guitarist/singer/bystander animations;
* a tutorial and a staged Chakra proof of concept including the bird/rotation idea;
* placeholder pixel art, parallax scenery, crowd variation via palette swapping,
  and Windows export configuration.

This is valuable technical and design validation. However, the sequencing, beat
maps, and animation cues are largely hard-coded per audio filename. The repository
does not show production-ready hub progression, dialogue, saves, menus/options,
song selection/completion flow, robust calibration/remapping, accessibility,
automated tests, or a reusable authoring pipeline. Existing art/audio appear to be
prototype assets and should not be assumed final or legally cleared. Accordingly,
the estimate gives the POC a modest head start (approximately 120–200 hours of
discovery/prototyping avoided), but retains time to refactor and validate its core.


COMMON / ONE-TIME DEVELOPMENT WORK

1. Preproduction and production planning                         60–90 hours
   Lock scope and song classification; narrative outline; level/asset matrix;
   technical plan; naming/import rules; risk register; milestones and cut list.

2. Production architecture and POC refactor                    100–150 hours
   Move song definitions, beat maps, cues, transitions, scoring, and unlock data
   into reusable resources; scene lifecycle; event/state system; debug tools;
   error handling; version migration from the prototype.

3. Rhythm/audio foundation and authoring tools                  100–150 hours
   Stable audio clock and pause/seek behavior; latency calibration; configurable
   judgement windows; miss handling; score/grade; chart/cue editor or import path;
   waveform/BPM helpers; loop/transition validation; debug song seeking.

4. Player, camera, controls, and accessibility                   70–110 hours
   Polish movement and camera; keyboard/controller parity; controller hot-plug;
   remapping; prompt glyph switching; vibration option; reduced flashes/shake;
   color-safe cues; text/readability and timing assists.

5. Festival hub and world progression                          130–190 hours
   Hub layout and traversal; interactables; collision/navigation; song entrances
   and returns; day/dusk/night/dawn states; availability gates; crowd spawning;
   final departure/end trigger; performance passes.

6. Dialogue, narrative, and character systems                   70–110 hours
   Conversation triggers and conditions; text presentation; speaker identity;
   branching/state flags where needed; main/minor dialogue implementation;
   cutscene blocking; localization-ready text pipeline.

7. UI, menus, saves, and settings                              100–145 hours
   Main/pause/options/credits; tutorial prompts; track intro/results/retry;
   completion tracking and high scores; autosave/versioning/recovery; audio,
   display, input, and accessibility settings; confirmation and error states.

8. Shared art/effects implementation                            55–90 hours
   Character/crowd generator integration; reusable particles, psychedelic shader
   stack, palettes, transitions, UI animation, lighting/time-of-day controls, and
   performance quality levels. Asset creation itself is not counted here.

9. Shared audio implementation                                  25–45 hours
   Festival ambience/music zones; buses and volume controls; SFX playback and
   variation; fades/ducking; loop checks and loudness consistency.

10. Release engineering, QA, and store preparation              90–140 hours
    Windows builds; clean-machine/controller/resolution testing; save and long-song
    soak tests; performance/accessibility checks; credits/licenses; itch.io page,
    screenshots/trailer capture, packaging, upload, and a small launch patch.

COMMON TOTAL                                                   900–1,300 hours
                                                               22.5–32.5 weeks

The category ranges deliberately overlap in practice; the total is the controlling
number, not a promise that every line item will independently hit its minimum.


PER-SONG ESTIMATES

SIMPLE SONG                                                    100–160 hours
                                                               2.5–4 weeks

Typical scope: one 8–9 minute song, mainly shared Guitar-Hero-like/autoscroll or
free-dance play; a small number of reusable scene variants; no unique perspective
or major mechanic; starts/ends cleanly at the festival.

  Concept, gameplay breakdown, beat/chart design                18–26 hours
  Chart/cue implementation and first playable                   18–28 hours
  Scene assembly, reusable visuals, transitions                 24–38 hours
  Animation/effect/audio integration                            16–26 hours
  Playtesting, tuning, performance and bug fixing               24–42 hours

COMPLEX SONG                                                   280–440 hours
                                                               7–11 weeks

Typical scope: bespoke visual journey comparable to Chakra, multiple seamless or
looping sections, festival-to-fantasy transitions, set pieces, special movement or
camera behavior, custom choreography, and a wordless narrative. This is development
and integration time; creating the large custom art inventory is separately an art
production commitment.

  Concept, storyboard, technical breakdown                      35–55 hours
  Song segmentation, loop prep, chart and cue design            35–55 hours
  Bespoke mechanics/set pieces and camera                       60–100 hours
  Level assembly, transitions, choreography                     55–85 hours
  Animation, VFX, shader and audio integration                  45–75 hours
  Repeated full-song tests, tuning, optimization, fixes         50–70 hours

The 8+ minute track length makes iteration expensive: one complete test pass plus
notes and fixes can consume 15–30 minutes. Every new perspective, controllable
character, boss-like encounter, or one-off mechanic should add roughly 40–100 hours
unless it replaces other complex content. A song between the two definitions should
be budgeted at 180–260 hours rather than forced into the simple category.

ART ASSET INVENTORY

The exact counts depend on final visual style and song concepts. Items marked
"per complex song" should be multiplied by the number of showcase songs.

Branding and storefront

* Final game logo and wordmark; light/dark variants.
* Windows executable icon at required sizes.
* itch.io cover, banner, thumbnail, page background, and animated GIF/video crops.
* Screenshot compositions and trailer title/end cards.

Player

* Male and female player base designs, or a modular body system.
* Idle, walk, run, jump, fall, land, dash, dance/beat, power move, and contextual
  interaction animations; facing/directional variants as required.
* Rhythm-state poses for freefall/flying/hanging and any alternate perspectives.
* Player customization parts if retained: skin tones, hair, faces, tops, bottoms,
  shoes/accessories, palettes, and portrait/speaker representation.
* Shadow, trail, hit/miss feedback, landing/dash particles, and silhouette variants.

Named characters

* Sahi, Kara, Barry, Unnamed, and Blackness final designs.
* For each festival character: idle, walk where needed, conversation, multiple dance
  loops, reactions/cheers, and story-specific gestures.
* Sahi car/end-scene poses and vehicle-seated variants.
* Dialogue portraits or speaker icons if portraits are chosen for the UI.
* Blackness shape language, transformations, masks, tendrils/particles, and bespoke
  interactions required by the showcase songs.

Festival crowd and performers

* Modular attendee bases spanning varied bodies, skin tones, hair, clothing,
  accessories, mobility aids if represented, and color palettes.
* Crowd idle/dance/cheer/sit/lie/walk loops; enough variants to avoid obvious clones.
* Musicians/DJs: drummer and kit, guitarist/instrument, singer/microphone, stage DJ
  and decks; performance animations synchronized to common beat divisions.
* Vendors, security/staff, one-off conversation NPCs, and campsite occupants.
* Crowd LOD/silhouette assets or batched background dancers for performance.

Festival environment

* Modular terrain/tiles: ground, paths, hill/cliffs, desert, shorelines, lake/water,
  docks and collision/edge variants.
* Entrance/gates, fences, signs, wristband/check-in props.
* Small and main stages, speaker stacks, lights, truss, decks, instruments, cables,
  barriers, banners, and dance-floor decoration.
* Tent/campsite kit: tents, canopies, rugs, chairs, bags, coolers, vehicles and clutter.
* Food court/vendor stalls, tables, menus/signage, bins, toilets and utility props.
* Large-lake boat and dock; small-lake dressing; car for the ending.
* Trees/plants/rocks, desert dressing, flags, shade structures, sculptures/totems,
  lanterns and festival art installations.
* Background/parallax layers for each region and horizon/sky/weather layers.
* Water animations/reflections, dust, smoke/fog, fire/light sources, insects/birds.
* Day, dusk, night and dawn palettes/lighting overlays; lit/unlit prop variants.

Shared rhythm presentation and VFX

* Beat-note icons for every supported input/action and direction.
* Target/receptor, approach trail, timing window, hit, miss, combo/grade, and
  power-move visuals; keyboard, mouse, Xbox-style and generic controller glyphs.
* Reusable particles: bursts, trails, sparks, confetti, glow, waves/rings, smoke,
  stars, clouds, leaves, water splashes, dust and abstract geometry.
* Reusable full-screen effects/masks/noise textures: palette shifts, distortion,
  kaleidoscope, mirroring, chromatic effects, rotation, tunnels and transitions.
* Reduced-flash/reduced-motion alternatives for intense effects.

UI

* Main, pause, options, controls, credits, save/error, confirmation and results
  layouts; panels, buttons, focus/hover/disabled states, sliders/toggles and cursors.
* Dialogue box, speaker name/portrait treatment, continue marker, talk indicator.
* Track intro/outro card; artist/title/length; score, grade, beats hit, high score,
  completion marker and retry prompt.
* Tutorial callouts and input glyph atlas.
* Font family with commercial license and required characters; fallback fonts if
  localization is planned.

Per simple song

* One visual theme/background set, usually 3–6 layers or modular scene pieces.
* 2–4 reusable obstacle/set-piece families and any rhythm-aligned animations.
* Song-specific palette, particles/texture accents, intro/outro transition, title art
  treatment, and small prop/enemy/character set as the concept requires.
* Rough planning count: 15–35 final sprites/elements plus shared VFX; more if heavily
  animated. Atlas frames are not counted as separate assets here.

Per complex song

* Storyboard/animatic and visual-development sheets.
* 3–6 distinct environment phases with backgrounds, foregrounds, terrain and props.
* Song-specific characters/creatures/vehicles and their animation sets.
* Unique set-piece assets and alternate player forms/perspectives where required.
* Bespoke transition art, full-screen effects, masks, particles and shader textures.
* Festival start/return variants that visually join the fantasy level to the hub.
* Rough planning count: 50–120+ final sprites/elements plus animation frames and VFX.
  Chakra specifically implies performers/instruments, expanded festival terrain,
  clouds, giant bird, freefall/flying scenery, rotation/hanging states, lake landing,
  crowd call-and-response, and all transitions between those phases.


AUDIO ASSET INVENTORY (EXCLUDING THE MUSIC LIST ITSELF)

Music preparation/deliverables for each licensed track

* Final lossless master at consistent sample rate/bit depth and agreed loudness.
* Exact BPM and tempo map, including tempo changes; downbeat/bar markers.
* Cleared artist name, track title, credits, copyright text, and license documentation.
* Clean loop files or edit points for every loop-until-location section.
* Transition/tail files so looping sections advance without clicks or lost reverb.
* Optional stems only where needed for festival distance treatment, adaptive fades,
  or a specifically designed miss effect; otherwise a final stereo master is enough.
* Lower-volume/distant festival version or a tested filter/mix preset.

Festival ambience

* Continuous crowd beds at quiet, medium and dense intensities.
* Distant stage/music wash suitable for use beneath conversation (original or fully
  licensed; do not assume the featured song license permits modified ambient use).
* Location loops: entrance, campsite/tents, desert/wind, small lake, large lake/shore,
  food court, stage vicinity, and dawn/night variants as useful.
* One-shot crowd cheers, shouts, laughter and call-and-response, with enough variants
  to avoid repetition.
* Water laps/splashes, wind, birds/insects, fire/generator/vehicle ambience as needed.

Character and movement SFX

* Footsteps for dirt/sand, grass, wood/stage, rock and shallow water; walk/run sets.
* Jump, land by intensity/surface, dash/whoosh, slide/duck if implemented, splash,
  swim/boat interactions, clothing movement and power-move effect.
* Nonverbal conversation "voice" blips or character-specific chatter sets, if used;
  separate sets for the main cast and generic attendees.
* Cheers, breaths/grunts, dance reactions and story-specific vocal one-shots.

World/UI SFX

* Interaction/talk prompt, dialogue advance/open/close, menu navigate/confirm/back,
  slider/toggle, pause/unpause, save complete, error/disabled, track unlocked,
  track start, results reveal, grade reveal, retry and game completion.
* Environmental one-shots: tent/flap, boat/dock creaks, water entry, car door/engine,
  props and any scripted festival interactions.
* The design calls for visual rather than audio feedback on rhythm hits/misses. If
  that changes, add subtle hit/miss/combo sounds and test carefully against music.

Audio production requirements

* Mix/master pass across songs, ambience and SFX; consistent perceived volume.
* Seamless-loop editing and click-free fades.
* Audio bus setup/assets for master, music, ambience, SFX and dialogue-blip controls.
* Credits and license records for every recording, library sound, and font/asset.


MAJOR RISKS AND SCOPE CONTROLS

* Song licensing and editable loop material are schedule gates. Clear each track and
  request loop/transition deliverables before building its level.
* Complex-song art is likely the largest production bottleneck. Lock storyboard and
  reuse plan before final asset creation; prototype every set piece with grey boxes.
* Long songs multiply testing time. Build in-editor seek, section restart, autoplay,
  chart validation and bot/input simulation early.
* Audio latency varies by hardware. Ship manual calibration and test Bluetooth,
  wired audio, several frame rates, and multiple controllers.
* Avoid adding unique mechanics casually. Treat each as a separately estimated
  feature and prefer remixing a small polished mechanic set.
* A 28-song game is possible but carries several years of solo content risk. Design
  the data pipeline so a polished seven-song release can later expand without
  committing the first commercial version to all 28.
* Keep cuts explicit. Good first cuts are alternate perspectives, deep character
  customization, branching dialogue, numerous interactable minor NPCs, and bespoke
  mechanics in simple songs—not calibration, remapping, save reliability, or QA.


RECOMMENDED PRODUCTION GATES FOR THE SEVEN-SONG VERSION

Gate 1 — Production foundation (roughly months 1–4)
  Refactor the POC, establish song data/authoring, calibration, saves/settings,
  final visual target, and a grey-box festival hub.

Gate 2 — Vertical slice (roughly months 5–7)
  Finish one representative portion of the hub, dialogue, one polished simple song,
  and a production-quality slice of Chakra with final-quality art/audio workflow.
  Re-estimate asset throughput from measured results before committing all content.

Gate 3 — Content production (roughly months 8–17)
  Produce the remaining songs, hub states, narrative, and assets in parallel batches;
  test each song continuously rather than deferring full playthroughs.

Gate 4 — Alpha to launch (roughly months 18–24)
  Content lock, complete end-to-end testing, accessibility/performance work, external
  playtests, fixes, store materials, release candidate, launch and first patch.

The month labels are planning targets for the recommended scope, not a replacement
for the hour ranges. After the vertical slice, use actual hours per finished minute
of simple and complex song content to rebuild the forecast.


BOTTOM LINE

For planning and funding, use 900–1,300 hours of shared production work, 100–160
hours for each simple song, and 280–440 hours for each complex song, then add 20%.
Start with seven songs and protect three showcase tracks. That yields a commercially
credible target of about 2,568–3,912 hours, or 15–23 working months at 40 hours/week;
plan approximately 18–24 calendar months and reassess after a final-quality vertical
slice. The estimate excludes the labor/cost of actually creating commissioned art,
audio, music, localization, and marketing, but the required art and non-music audio
deliverables are inventoried above.
