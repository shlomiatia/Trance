============================================================
A. SELF-REVIEW — CORRECTIONS AND ADDITIONS TO v1
============================================================

Things others included that v1 missed or under-weighted:

1. ACCESSIBILITY (from GPT 5.6) — v1 omitted it entirely. For a deliberately psychedelic,
   screen-rotating, flashing game this is not optional for a commercial release:
   reduced-flash/reduced-motion alternatives, color-safe beat cues, photosensitivity care.
   ADD: ~30–50 h to common work, plus "reduced-effect variants" as an art line item.

2. LATENCY CALIBRATION AS A FIRST-CLASS FEATURE (GPT 5.6, mentioned in passing in v1).
   Audio latency varies wildly (Bluetooth headphones especially). A rhythm game needs a
   manual calibration screen and testing across audio devices/frame rates. Also: the POC's
   delta-accumulated `_playback_timer` will drift over an 8-minute track — the rewrite must
   use the AudioServer clock. v1 said this; keeping it, with more emphasis.

3. TESTING TOOLS FOR LONG SONGS (GPT 5.6) — an 8.5-minute track makes every tuning pass
   expensive. In-editor seek, section restart, autoplay/bot playback, and chart validation
   belong in the tooling budget. The POC's ESC-to-skip is the embryo of this.
   ADD: ~20–30 h to the tooling line (tooling: 60–110 h total).

4. MUSIC DELIVERABLES SPEC (GPT 5.6 — best catch in any doc). For EACH licensed track you
   need, from the artist/label or your own DAW work: lossless master at consistent loudness,
   BPM/tempo map with downbeat markers, click-free loop files with reverb tails for every
   looping section, a low-volume/filtered "festival PA" version — and note the license may
   not automatically permit modified/ambient use of the track. Fold this into the licensing
   conversation NOW, not after signing.

5. MID-TIER SONG CATEGORY (GPT 5.6). Some tracks will be "simple plus one gimmick" and cost
   between the two buckets. Budget any such track at roughly 2× a simple track rather than
   forcing it into the simple bucket. Every casually-added unique mechanic is +40–100 h.

6. COMPLEX-TRACK COUNT SCALES WITH SET SIZE (Opus 5; Gemini Pro independently used 4
   complex at 14 tracks). The doc's "at least 3" is a floor. At 14 tracks expect 3–4
   complex; at 28 expect 5–6. v1's larger-scenario totals assumed a fixed 3; corrected
   in Part C.

7. POST-LAUNCH TRACKS ARE CHEAP (Opus 5) — good commercial framing: after shipping, each
   additional simple track is ~1.5–2 weeks. Ship 7, sell, then expand — content updates are
   incremental work, not a rebuild. Also from Opus: consider the Dialogic plugin instead of
   a from-scratch dialogue system (could save ~30–50 h).

8. WORKING MONTHS ≠ CALENDAR MONTHS (GPT 5.6). Hour totals convert to full-time weeks, but
   vacations, illness, admin, artist-turnaround waits stretch calendar time ~15–25%.
   v1's "12–13 months" was working time; the calendar number is longer.

9. RE-ESTIMATE AFTER A VERTICAL SLICE (GPT 5.6's production gates). After finishing one
   simple track + a production-quality Chakra slice on the new engine, measure actual
   hours-per-track and rebuild the forecast. That measurement is worth more than all six
   of these documents combined.

10. OUTSOURCE CASH BUDGET (Sonnet 5 itemized it per-track). Sonnet's total ($8k–19k) and
    v1's ($12k–40k) bracket the truth; per-track art framing is useful for briefs:
    complex track ≈ $800–2,000 of bespoke art; simple track ≈ $150–350. Get real quotes.

11. SMALL ADDITIONS to asset lists from others: controller hot-plug handling; vibration
    option; portraits with 2–3 expressions per speaking character; score-tally count-up
    SFX; wind SFX for freefall; giant-bird cry; crowd call-and-response one-shots
    (Chakra part 7); parked cars incl. Sahi's car as a hub prop; post-launch patch
    allowance (~20–40 h).

12. MIDI-IMPORT CHARTING OPTION (Gemini Flash — its one standout idea). Instead of (or
    before) building a full waveform-timeline editor UI, author beat charts as MIDI in a
    DAW against the actual track and import them (note pitch = direction, note time =
    beat time). For a music-literate developer this could cut the editor build to an
    importer + in-game preview/nudge tool, and DAW editing beats any homemade timeline UI.
    Worth prototyping FIRST — if it works, it trims 30–60 h off the tooling line and
    speeds every mapping pass after. Flash also usefully quantifies chart density:
    an 8.5-min psytrance track at 130–145 BPM is ~800–1,500 mappable beats — that's the
    real reason hand-typed dictionaries (the POC approach) cannot scale.

Corrections in the other direction (v1 things I'm keeping despite disagreement elsewhere):
- v1's prominent MUSIC LICENSING warning stays — only GPT 5.6 treated it as the schedule
  gate it is. It remains the single biggest external risk to a commercial release.
- v1's recommendation to CUT the procedural character generator to hand-made variants
  stays (Opus and Sonnet independently flagged it as the classic scope trap; Gemini Pro
  budgets a "simple modular NPC generator" inside a 2-week line, which is exactly how the
  trap starts).

Net effect on v1's numbers: common work rises modestly (accessibility, calibration
emphasis, testing tools, post-launch patch: roughly +70–120 h), and the headline gains a
working-vs-calendar distinction. Per-song numbers hold.

Critique per model:

GPT 5.6 — The most thorough by far: accessibility, calibration, testing tools, music
  deliverables, production gates, working-vs-calendar months, post-launch patch. Its asset
  inventory is effectively a ready commissioning brief. Its numbers are the highest;
  280–440 h per complex track is defensible if you count the brutal iteration cost of
  8.5-minute tracks, but the low end of its ranges is probably the honest center. Slight
  systematic padding (it double-counts some iteration in both per-track "testing" and the
  20% contingency). Treat it as the P80 (conservative planning) estimate.

Opus 5 — Best structured: clean formula (common + per-track × count − Chakra credit
  + 30% contingency), correct scaling of complex-track count with set size, sharp read of
  the POC ("most of the risk, minority of the work"), actionable build order, and the
  post-launch-content framing. Numbers sit centrally among the strong models. Weakness:
  thin on licensing and accessibility. Treat it as the P50 (median) estimate.

Fable 5 (v1) — Central numbers, strongest on licensing risk, artist-budget ballpark and
  the audio-engineering work hidden in "you have the music" (slicing, PA mixdowns). Missed
  accessibility and calibration-as-feature; both fixed above.

Gemini Pro 3.1 — Credible and well-aligned on per-track costs: 280 h per complex track
  matches GPT's low end exactly, and 80 h per simple track sits mid-cluster. Sensible
  scenario math and an explicit 15–20% buffer note (its "~11 months" headline is
  pre-buffer; ~12 working months buffered). Weaknesses are all in the common bucket —
  at ~640 h it's the leanest credible foundation: beat tooling is folded into a 4-week
  "rhythm foundation" line, dialogue system + NPC generator share 2 weeks, UI/menus get
  2 weeks, and there's no accessibility, calibration, licensing treatment, or Chakra POC
  credit. Week-level granularity also hides where the risk lives. Treat it as ~P30 —
  believable if the common systems go unusually smoothly.

Sonnet 5 — Sensible structure and a useful outsource dollar budget, but light: ~160 h per
  complex track undercounts iteration on an 8.5-minute bespoke level (the POC's Chakra —
  shorter and placeholder-quality — plausibly consumed a large share of that already), and
  ~910 h common is lean for commercial-grade menus/options/QA/release. Its 9–13 months
  reads as the optimistic edge of plausible. Treat as P20 (best case).

Gemini Flash 3.6 (~5.7 months) — Not credible for a COMMERCIAL release. It prices a
  game-jam build of the feature list: 270 h for ALL core systems is less than the strong
  models allocate to the rhythm-engine rewrite plus tooling alone; 85 h per complex track
  doesn't survive contact with the Chakra design's 14 scripted parts; a complete dialogue
  system in 30 h and full UI/menus/save/release stack in ~90 h are jam numbers; and it
  carries no contingency while claiming "high polish" in 6 months. Weight its totals near
  zero. That said, it contributes real ideas the others missed: the MIDI-import charting
  pipeline (Part A item 12), the beats-per-track quantification (~800–1,500 per song),
  and a low-pass/dampening miss effect that fits the design's no-rhythm-SFX pillar better
  than a "miss thud" would.

Consensus across ALL six (worth trusting because it's unanimous):
  - The POC de-risks the concept but is hardcoded; a data-driven rewrite is prerequisite #1.
  - A beat-charting pipeline (editor tool and/or MIDI import) is on the critical path
    before content production can start.
  - The procedural character generator is the #1 scope trap — timebox or cut to hand-made
    modular variants.
  - Ship the 7-track set first (Gemini Pro and Flash now say this explicitly too); more
    tracks are cheap after the systems exist.
  - The "no fail state / no rhythm SFX / minimal UI" pillars genuinely reduce scope — the
    SFX list is almost entirely festival-side.

============================================================
C. COMBINED BEST GUESS (weighted synthesis)
============================================================

Weighting: GPT 5.6, Opus 5, Fable 5 heavily (they independently converge); Gemini Pro 3.1
and Sonnet 5 moderately (they define the credible optimistic band at ~10–12 working
months); Gemini Flash ~zero on totals (methodologically unsound for commercial scope,
as argued above), though its tooling ideas are adopted.

PLANNING FORMULA (developer hours, before contingency):
  Common / one-time:         1,000–1,500 h
  Per complex track:           200–350 h   (Chakra: ~120–200 net of the POC)
  Per mid-tier track:          120–180 h   (simple + one unique gimmick)
  Per simple track:             60–110 h
  Contingency:                 +20–25% on the sum (solo overrun base rate)

7 TRACKS (3 complex + 4 simple) — RECOMMENDED FIRST RELEASE:
  Pre-contingency:   ~1,900–2,700 h
  With contingency:  ~2,300–3,300 h
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  BEST GUESS:        ~2,500 h  ≈  62 weeks  ≈  14–15 working months
  CALENDAR PLAN:     ~16–18 months (working time + vacations/illness/
                     admin/artist-turnaround gaps)
  Optimistic (everything goes right):   ~11–12 months
  Conservative (planning/funding case): ~22 months
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  (Five credible estimates land at roughly 1,855 / 2,100 / 2,300 / 2,440 / 2,568–3,912 h.
   ~2,500 h sits just above the median — the median leans optimistic because the lighter
   estimates also budget the least for release-grade polish, accessibility, and QA.)

14 TRACKS (3–4 complex + 10–11 simple): ~3,300–4,600 h → ~20–26 working months
28 TRACKS (5–6 complex + 22–23 simple): ~4,800–6,800 h → ~29–39 working months
  (Confirms every credible model's advice: do NOT target these first. Ship 7, then expand —
   each post-launch simple track is only ~1.5–2.5 weeks once the pipeline exists.)

EXTERNAL BUDGET (parallel cash stream, not dev hours):
  Commissioned higher-fidelity pixel art per the v1 inventory: ~$10k–$35k
  (500–900 artist-hours; controllable via: hand-made NPC variants instead of a generator,
  palette-swap time-of-day instead of redrawn states, licensed font, batched briefs).
  SFX: small — a good commercial SFX library subscription or ~$300–1,000 commissioned.
  Music licensing: unknown until negotiated; START NOW — it is the only item that can
  invalidate the whole plan, and each track's license should come with the deliverables
  spec from Part A item 4.

WHY TRUST ~14–15 WORKING MONTHS: four independent estimates using different methods
(bottom-up hours, formula + 30%, week-based scenarios + buffer, inventory + 20%) land at
13–14, ~14, ~12, and 15–23 working months. The spread between them is mostly contingency
philosophy plus how much release-grade polish they budget, not disagreement about the
work itself. The number to publish is 16–18 calendar months; the number that beats all of
these is your own measured throughput after the vertical slice (build the new engine,
re-fit Chakra, finish one simple track — then re-forecast from actuals).

SUGGESTED BUILD ORDER (unchanged from v1, endorsed by Opus and GPT independently):
  1. Rhythm engine rewrite (data-driven, AudioServer clock, calibration) + beat charting
     pipeline — prototype the MIDI-import route first, fall back to a custom editor.
  2. Re-fit Chakra onto it with placeholder art — proves the pipeline end-to-end.
  3. Festival hub + dialogue + time-of-day (the completely untested half — find the
     unknowns early).
  4. Vertical-slice checkpoint: measure hours/track, re-estimate, THEN commission art
     in batches.
  5. Remaining tracks in parallel with art integration.
  6. Menus/save/options/accessibility/release engineering + polish (final ~2–3 months).
